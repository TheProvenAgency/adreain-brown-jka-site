The 3 Documents That Start Every Funding File

AB Adreain Brown · JKA Consulting · Credit Strategy

Short answer: every funding and credit file starts with three documents — a government-issued photo ID, a Social Security card, and one proof of address. Nothing moves until these three are verified.

1. Government-Issued Photo ID

A driver's license, passport, or state-issued ID confirms identity and matches the name on any application submitted on your behalf. Mismatched or expired IDs are one of the most common reasons a file gets delayed before it ever reaches a lender.

2. Social Security Card

This confirms the Social Security number tied to your credit profile is accurate before any dispute or funding application is filed. Submitting the wrong number, or a number that doesn't match your ID, is one of the fastest ways to have a file rejected outright.

3. Proof of Address

A recent utility bill, bank statement, or pay stub confirms your current address matches what's on file with the bureaus and lenders. Address mismatches are a common, avoidable reason applications get flagged for manual review, which slows down approval timelines that would otherwise take under 48 hours.

Why This Comes First

These three documents exist to confirm we're working the right profile, accurately, from day one. Skipping or rushing this step doesn't save time — it costs it, because inaccurate information at the start means disputes get filed against the wrong details or applications get submitted with data that doesn't match what the bank has on record.

"Providing accurate and up-to-date information allows us to dispute negative items effectively and improve your credit standing."

Getting these three right the first time is what makes a 48-hour approval possible in the first place — it's the difference between a file that moves the same day it's submitted and one that sits in review.
