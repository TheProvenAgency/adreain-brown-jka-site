Personal Loan to Business Credit: How to Make Debt Disappear

AB Adreain Brown · JKA Consulting · Credit Strategy

Short answer: revolving credit card utilization drags a credit score down in a way that installment debt, like a personal loan, does not. Using a personal loan to pay off maxed-out credit cards — then paying that loan off fast using business funding — is one of the quickest legitimate ways to jump a score 70 to 80 points.

The Core Insight

Credit scoring models weigh revolving utilization heavily. A card that's 50% maxed out signals risk even if every payment has been made on time. A personal loan reports differently — it's an installment product, and it doesn't carry the same utilization penalty. Paying off $25,000 in card debt with a personal loan doesn't just move the balance, it removes the utilization hit entirely.

Step by Step

Take out a personal loan sized to cover the existing credit card debt (commonly through SoFi, Best Egg, or Prosper). Use it to pay the cards down to zero. Within 30 days, the reporting updates and utilization drops, which is typically enough to move a score from the high 700s into the 800+ range — the tier where banks compete for a borrower instead of the other way around.

Then Make The Loan Disappear

The personal loan itself doesn't need to sit on the profile long-term. Once business funding comes through from the accounts opened and seasoned afterward, that business credit is used to pay off the personal loan in full — often within 30 to 45 days of opening it. A loan that's opened and paid off quickly, in full, builds a stronger relationship with that lender than one carried for years.

Why Not Just Use The Cards Directly?

Because utilization is the fastest lever a score has, and it moves in both directions. Carrying high balances on revolving cards, even while paying business debt down elsewhere, keeps utilization elevated and caps how much new funding a profile can unlock. Clearing it first via installment debt, then rebuilding business credit fresh, produces a cleaner and faster path to larger approvals.

"I believe in credit buildout over credit score."

The score jump is real, but it's a byproduct — the actual goal is buildout: more available credit, spread across more relationships, that can be responsibly leveraged into the next round of funding.
