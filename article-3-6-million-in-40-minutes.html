$3.6 Million in Approvals in 40 Minutes: Inside a Live Funding Call

AB Adreain Brown · JKA Consulting · Funding Strategy

Short answer: $3.6 million in business funding approvals came through in 40 minutes on a single live call — not from the biggest audience ever hosted, but from the smallest and most engaged one.

A Conversation On The Way Out The Door

The connection that led to the call started small: a conversation with a woman named Janice, met literally on her way out the exit of the Investfest conference. That short conversation turned into a follow-up, and by Wednesday of the following week, Janice had gone from a curious conversation to someone building her own funding business — and personally landed $250,000 in approvals of her own.

The Call Itself

On that same Wednesday, a live funding call produced $3.6 million in approvals in 40 minutes. What made it notable wasn't just the number — it was the room size. Most funding communities run these live calls with 200 to 300 people watching, and typically hesitant to apply live in front of a crowd. This call had the fewest attendees of any live funding session run to date, and it produced the most funding ever seen on one.

The takeaway wasn't that a smaller audience is inherently better — it's that engagement, trust, and people actually willing to apply live matters more than raw headcount. A packed room that watches is worth less than a small room that acts.

Turning Down The Sale, Not The Person

Earlier the same week, during a live challenge session, a woman shared that she was in the process of losing her home and had spent her last $25 on the mentorship because of a video she'd seen days earlier. That moment became a test that had nothing to do with revenue: rather than continuing to sell, the mentorship was given to her for free, on the spot.

That decision came without consulting a team or running the math on what it cost the launch. It was a judgment call made in real time, and it's part of what shaped the tone of the entire week — that the mission came before the margin.

Watching The Number Climb

Hours later, on the same emotionally heavy day, a sales call opened with a simple offer: 25 spots, first come, first served, link live for a limited window. Within minutes of the link going out, refreshing the payment dashboard showed the total jump to $57,000. A second refresh, still within that same five-minute window, showed it climbing toward $75,000–$77,000.

That single day — giving away a spot for free in the morning and watching tens of thousands of dollars come in within minutes that same evening — became the run-up to the biggest single-day result of the year.

"It's a numbers game."

The $3.6 million wasn't the product of the biggest promotion or the largest list. It came from a small, trusting room, a relationship that started with a hallway conversation, and a willingness to give something away for free hours before asking a much larger group to buy in.
